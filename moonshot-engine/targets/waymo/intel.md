### Tech Stack

**6th-Generation Hardware (current, began autonomous ops Feb 2026):**
- **13 cameras** (down from 29 in 5th gen)
- **4 lidar sensors** (down from 5)
- **6 radar units**
- External Audio Receivers (EARs)
- Custom silicon chips for on-vehicle processing
- **Hardware cost target: sub-$20,000** per unit (50%+ reduction from 5th gen)
- Deployed on: Zeekr-built "Ojai" minivan and Hyundai IONIQ 5

**5th-Generation Hardware (still in fleet):**
- 29 cameras, 5 lidar, 6 imaging radar
- 360-degree lidar + perimeter lidar for close-range
- Long-range camera sees 500m; imaging radar for velocity detection in bad weather
- Deployed on: Jaguar I-PACE

**AI/ML Architecture:**
- **Modular pipeline:** Sensor fusion -> Perception (object detection/tracking) -> Prediction (trajectory forecasting) -> Planning (driving decisions) -> Control (actuators)
- **HD Maps + Geofencing:** Vehicles operate in pre-mapped Operational Design Domains (ODD) using lidar-built 3D maps
- **Waymo Foundation Model:** Modular transformer-based neural network combining AV-specific AI with LLM/VLM "world knowledge" and reasoning
- **EMMA (End-to-End Multimodal Model):** Research-stage model mapping raw camera inputs directly to driving outputs; uses chain-of-thought reasoning; 6.7% improvement in planning performance
- **RL in the Planner:** Multiple published papers on using reinforcement learning to fine-tune driving planners:
  - "Imitation Is Not Enough" -- combining imitation learning with RL for challenging scenarios
  - "Improving Agent Behaviors with RL Fine-tuning" (CoRL 2024) -- closed-loop RL fine-tuning of behavior models
  - **Waymax simulator** -- accelerated data-driven simulator with RL training interfaces (Brax API, dm-env API)
- Remote Assistance: ~70 agents on duty worldwide at any time (US + Philippines); they provide guidance but do not drive

Sources: [Waymo blog - 6th gen](https://waymo.com/blog/2024/08/meet-the-6th-generation-waymo-driver/), [Waymo blog - AI/ML](https://waymo.com/blog/2024/10/ai-and-ml-at-waymo/), [Waymo Research](https://waymo.com/research/)

### Where Waymo Is Winning
1. **Safety record:** 90% fewer serious injury crashes vs human drivers over 127M rider-only miles ([Waymo Safety Impact](https://waymo.com/safety/impact/))
2. **Commercial scale:** Only company with 10+ operational cities and 450K+ weekly paid rides
3. **Regulatory relationships:** Deep trust built over 15+ years; first-mover in permitting across multiple states
4. **Rider experience:** Consistently high NPS; genuine delight factor for new riders
5. **Research depth:** 70+ published papers; foundation model, EMMA, Waymax simulator
6. **Alphabet backing:** Functionally unlimited capital; $16B raised in single round

### Where Waymo Is Losing
1. **Cost per ride:** $1.98/mile vs Tesla's projected $0.81/mile; 30-40% premium over Uber/Lyft
2. **Hardware cost:** $75K-$150K per vehicle vs Baidu's RT6 at ~$28K; Chinese lidar units at <$200 vs Waymo's custom sensors
3. **Scaling speed:** Phoenix took 5 years to mature; Austin at 8% of rides after 9 months; new cities start slow
4. **International:** Zero commercial international operations (London/Tokyo testing only); Chinese competitors already in Dubai, planning UK/Germany
5. **Geofence dependency:** Cannot operate outside pre-mapped areas; Tesla's camera-only approach could theoretically go anywhere
6. **Consumer perception headwinds:** School bus violations, child incident in Santa Monica, SF power outage failures generate negative headlines
7. **Path to profitability unclear:** At $126B valuation and ~$355M revenue, trading at 355x revenue; may remain "severely unprofitable at significant scale" before unit economics work
8. **Andrej Karpathy's critique:** "Tesla may still have a software problem, but Waymo has a hardware problem. Between the two, software is much easier to solve."

## 6. Sujoy-Specific Opportunity Mapping

### Mapping 1: RL in Production -> Waymo's Planner

**Sujoy's experience:** At Duetto, replaced batch linear programming with real-time RL inference for hotel pricing -- billions of daily pricing decisions, 10% RevPAR lift, 20% cost reduction, 10% latency reduction.

**Why this maps directly to Waymo:**
Waymo's planner is the single most critical component of the Waymo Driver -- it decides what the car does next. Waymo has published multiple papers explicitly on using RL to improve the planner:
- "Imitation Is Not Enough: Robustifying Imitation with Reinforcement Learning for Challenging Driving Scenarios" -- their core approach is to use RL to handle edge cases that pure imitation learning cannot cover
- "Improving Agent Behaviors with RL Fine-tuning for Autonomous Driving" (CoRL 2024) -- closed-loop RL fine-tuning of behavior models to reduce collision rates

Both problems share the same fundamental structure: **real-time optimization under uncertainty with hard safety constraints**. In Duetto's case, the safety constraint is revenue guarantees and rate parity. In Waymo's case, it is physical safety. The inference latency requirements are comparable (sub-100ms decisions). Sujoy's experience deploying RL that makes billions of real-time decisions in production -- not in a lab -- is exactly what Waymo needs as they scale from 450K to 1M+ weekly rides across 20+ cities.

**Specific talking point:** "The planner is making continuous real-time decisions under uncertainty with hard safety constraints -- that is structurally identical to what I built at Duetto, where our RL agent made billions of pricing decisions daily with revenue safety bounds. The difference is the constraint domain, not the architecture."

### Mapping 2: The Optimizer Pattern -> Waymo's Safe RL Deployment

**Sujoy's experience:** Used RL as an optimizer over a pretrained model at Duetto. "500 years of exploration in minutes" via pre-computed caches. Proved parity with existing approaches before expanding capabilities.

**Why this maps directly to Waymo:**
This is precisely how Waymo deploys RL in their planner. Their paper "Imitation Is Not Enough" describes using RL to *robustify* an imitation learning baseline -- not replace it. The imitation-learned policy is the "pretrained model," and RL fine-tunes it for challenging scenarios. The key insight Sujoy brings is the production discipline of **proving parity before expanding the decision space** -- Waymo cannot afford to deploy an RL-enhanced planner that performs worse on 99% of scenarios even if it handles the 1% edge cases better.

**Specific talking point:** "At Duetto, we proved that the RL optimizer matched the existing linear programming baseline on all standard scenarios before we let it improve on edge cases. This 'prove parity, then expand' discipline is exactly what Waymo needs when deploying RL fine-tuning over imitation learning baselines -- you cannot regress on normal driving to improve on challenging scenarios."

### Mapping 3: Agentic AI with Explainability -> Rider & Regulator Trust

**Sujoy's experience:** At Duetto, increased pricing acceptance from 20% to 60%+ by solving trust through transparency -- making the AI's decisions explainable to hotel revenue managers.

**Why this maps directly to Waymo:**
Waymo faces a **trust engineering problem at multiple levels**:
1. **Rider trust:** Passengers need to understand why the car is doing what it is doing (e.g., why it stopped, why it chose this route, why it is going slower than traffic). The geofence trap incident on Miami's MacArthur Causeway and the SF power outage demonstrate that when riders cannot understand or predict the vehicle's behavior, trust collapses.
2. **Regulator trust:** The Feb 2026 Senate testimony on remote assistance workers, the school bus violations, and the Santa Monica child incident all require Waymo to explain its decision-making to regulators in interpretable terms. Waymo's EMMA model explicitly uses "chain-of-thought reasoning" to provide interpretable rationale for driving decisions -- this is the same explainability pattern.
3. **City/community trust:** Every new city launch is a trust-building exercise. SF Supervisor Jackie Fielder's comment ("If these vehicles can't handle a storm, they should not be on our streets") shows how quickly community trust can erode.

Sujoy's experience taking acceptance from 20% to 60%+ by making AI decisions transparent is directly transferable. The PM, Driving Behaviors role is literally about defining how the car behaves -- and making those behaviors trusted by riders, regulators, and cities.

**Specific talking point:** "Trust is not a feature you ship once -- it is a continuous product surface. At Duetto, I increased AI acceptance from 20% to 60% by engineering transparency into every decision. Waymo's challenge is structurally identical: riders in the back seat, regulators reviewing crash data, and city councils voting on permits all need to trust the Waymo Driver's decisions."

### Mapping 4: Batch -> Real-Time Infrastructure Migration -> Waymo's Inference Stack

**Sujoy's experience:** At Duetto, migrated from batch to real-time infrastructure with 4x optimization, 20% cost reduction, 10% latency reduction.

**Why this maps directly to Waymo:**
Waymo is in the middle of its own infrastructure transition with the 6th-gen Driver. They have cut sensor count by 42% and are targeting 50%+ cost reduction per hardware unit -- but the software must do *more with less*. The 6th-gen system pushes more processing into custom silicon and requires the ML stack to extract equivalent performance from fewer cameras and lidar units. This is fundamentally the same optimization challenge: **maintain or improve output quality while dramatically reducing computational cost and latency**.

Additionally, as Waymo scales from 3,000 to potentially 10,000+ vehicles, the fleet-level inference, monitoring, and remote assistance infrastructure must scale proportionally. The current 70 remote assistance agents handling 3,000 vehicles will not scale linearly to 10,000+.

**Specific talking point:** "Transitioning from the 5th-gen to 6th-gen Driver is architecturally similar to what I did at Duetto -- extracting equal or better performance from a leaner infrastructure. The 42% sensor reduction means the ML models must compensate with better inference, and the cost reduction from $40K+ to sub-$20K per Driver unit requires the same kind of systems optimization I delivered (4x optimization, 20% cost reduction)."

### Mapping 5: 0-to-1 Product Development -> New City Launches

**Sujoy's experience:** Built 3 product lines from scratch at Duetto; built iOS Safari Extension at Capital One from 0 to 4M users.

**Why this maps directly to Waymo:**
Every new city Waymo enters is a **0-to-1 product launch**. The PM, Driving Behaviors role explicitly states: "Work on AI/ML and Planning technologies that enable Waymo's Driver to scale in domestic and international markets." Each city has unique:
- Road infrastructure and driving norms (Nashville has different challenges than London)
- Regulatory environments (Illinois has made robotaxis illegal; London requires DVSA approval)
- Weather conditions (Denver is Waymo's coldest city; London has persistent rain/fog)
- Cultural expectations (Tokyo left-hand traffic; UK roundabouts)
- The Waymo Driver's *behavior* must be tuned for each market -- this is the Driving Behaviors PM's job

Waymo went from 6 to 10 cities in one quarter and plans to add 10+ more in 2026. Each launch requires the same 0-to-1 discipline: define the MVP behavior set, prove safety, gain permits, launch, iterate.

**Specific talking point:** "I have built three product lines from zero at Duetto and scaled a consumer product from 0 to 4M users at Capital One. Each Waymo city launch is a 0-to-1 problem -- you need to define the minimum viable driving behavior set, prove safety equivalence, navigate local regulations, and then scale. I have done this exact pattern repeatedly."

### Mapping 6: Supply Chain Optimization -> Fleet Management & Routing

**Sujoy's experience:** At Wayfair, built a constraint-based routing engine for 20M+ shipments across 20K+ suppliers; pivoted from dashboard to optimization engine.

**Why this maps directly to Waymo:**
Waymo's fleet operations are a large-scale constrained optimization problem:
- **Vehicle routing:** Which vehicles serve which rides? How do you minimize deadheading (empty miles) across 3,000+ vehicles?
- **Charging optimization:** Electric vehicles need charging schedules that minimize downtime while ensuring coverage
- **Depot operations:** Cleaning, maintenance, and repair scheduling across multiple facilities in each city
- **Multi-partner coordination:** Uber dispatches some vehicles, Lyft dispatches others, Waymo dispatches its own -- routing logic must account for multiple demand sources
- **Supply-demand matching:** Repositioning vehicles to anticipated demand areas (similar to Wayfair's supplier-to-customer matching)

The pivot from "dashboard to optimization engine" at Wayfair mirrors Waymo's evolution from "research project to commercial fleet operation." The constraint structures are analogous: capacity constraints (vehicles), time windows (ride requests), geographic constraints (geofences/service areas), and cost minimization.

**Specific talking point:** "At Wayfair, I built a constraint-based routing engine optimizing 20M+ shipments across 20K+ suppliers. Waymo's fleet management problem is structurally similar -- you are matching a constrained supply of vehicles to demand across geofenced service areas, with charging, maintenance, and multi-partner dispatch constraints. I know how to think about these problems and, critically, how to pivot from dashboards to optimization engines."
