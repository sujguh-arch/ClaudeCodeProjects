# Business POV Prompts — Depth Forcing Function

**The business POV paragraph is the whole letter.** If it reads like a description of the company, the letter is dead. It has to read like a memo from someone who's been thinking about them.

Before writing, answer these out loud (or in a scratch file). Do not write the letter until you can answer all four.

---

## The Four Questions

### 1. What's the thesis behind their next 18 months?
Not their mission statement. What's the actual bet they're making about the market? If they win, what had to be true?

Good answers are specific: "They're betting that B2B buyers will tolerate agentic workflows sooner than consumers." Bad answers are generic: "They're betting on AI."

### 2. What's the non-obvious risk?
The thing a smart insider would say over drinks, not the thing on the earnings call. Usually it's a second-order effect of their strategy — a customer segment they'll lose, a moat that erodes, a platform dependency.

### 3. What would you actually do differently?
Not "I'd hire more engineers." Something concrete: a product surface you'd kill, a segment you'd push into, a pricing move, a distribution channel. The test: could you defend it against a pushback from someone senior?

### 4. Why you, specifically, for this?
Not your resume. The overlap between their next bet and something you've actually done. If you can't name a shipped thing from your past that directly maps, the letter is for a different role.

---

## Research inputs (don't skip)

Before answering the four questions, skim at minimum:
- The company's last 3 product launches (blog / changelog)
- Their latest funding or earnings narrative (what they told investors)
- 1 competitor's recent moves (where's the pressure coming from?)
- 1 critical take — TechCrunch dissenter, HN thread, competitor CEO tweet

You should be able to cite something from research the letter that the company's hiring manager would recognize as "this person actually looked."

---

## Calibration: signal vs. presumption

The POV paragraph walks a tightrope:
- **Too cautious** → reads like restating their website
- **Too aggressive** → reads like a stranger telling a founder how to run their company

The move is to frame as hypothesis, not declaration:
- "The bet I'd push harder on is..."
- "If I were framing this internally, I'd argue..."
- "The risk I'd be watching is..."
- "Where I'd lean is..."

Hedged-claim verbs are the register. Not "you should" — "I'd".

---

## The test

Read the POV paragraph to someone who doesn't know the company. Can they tell which company it's about from the paragraph alone? If no — it's generic. Rewrite.
